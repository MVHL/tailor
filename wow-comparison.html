Ways of Working — Current vs Proposed

How an epic travels from idea to "ready" today, versus the proposed problem-framed pipeline. The current flow and its pain points are taken from the refinement proposal; the proposed flow adds traceable artifacts and distributes the work off the squad lead.

Today Current WoW

Scrum intake, serial & lead-centric

  1. 1
    Feature intake
    BA discusses the new feature one-on-one with the squad lead.
    BA + Lead
  2. 2
    Exploration & feasibility
    Squad lead alone explores current app behavior and gives feedback.
    Lead only
  3. 3
    PRD written
    BA wraps the solution into a PRD (prose) and presents it to the team for feedback.
    BA
  4. 4
    One big refinement meeting
    Team estimates every ticket in one sitting → epic marked "ready" for the sprint.
    Whole team
  • 🔴
    Single point of failure. Exploration, feasibility & estimation all wait on the lead.
  • 🔴
    Serial & meeting-heavy. Each stage waits for the last; gaps surface late as rework.
  • 🔴
    One big batch. Breadth over depth → nominal estimates with no shared understanding.
  • 🔴
    Unstructured tickets. PRD prose; no explicit Problem → Requirement → AC trace.
Proposed New WoW

Problem-framed, parallel & distributed

  1. 1
    PRP — frame the problem
    Capture the problem(s) up front: who's affected, impact, success metric.
    BA + Lead
  2. 2
    Three Amigos triage
    BA + 1 dev + 1 tester (30–45 min) draft requirements and check feasibility — early, without the lead.
    3 Amigos
  3. 3
    PRD — sharpened & traceable
    Canonical, IDed Problem → Requirements → Non-Goals.
    BA
  4. 4
    Split into Stories + Tech tickets
    Each story owns a requirement slice with testable AC that traces upward.
    Team
  5. 5
    Continuous refinement
    ~10% capacity ongoing; async estimation; live meeting short & decision-only. Lead = escalation, not default path.
    Team (async)
  6. 6
    Implementation
    Test Plan ∥ Implementation Plan written from AC, in parallel → Code → QA → Release.
    Dev + Tester
  • 🟢
    No single channel. Triage + rotation spread the work; the lead is freed for delivery.
  • 🟢
    Parallel & early. Feasibility caught in triage; tests start before code.
  • 🟢
    Depth, continuously. Per-ticket framing + async estimates build shared understanding.
  • 🟢
    Traceable. Every requirement traces to a problem; every AC covers a requirement — no orphans, no gaps.

What the change buys you

Each structural problem in today's flow maps to a specific mechanism in the new one — with a metric to confirm it's actually working.

Today's problem What changes Benefit Signal it's working
Single point of failure — all refinement waits on the lead Three Amigos triage + rotated exploration/facilitation; lead becomes an escalation point Resilient intake that doesn't stall when the lead is heads-down on delivery lead's hours: facilitation vs development
Serial & meeting-heavy — gaps surface late as rework Feasibility resolved early in triage; Test Plan ∥ Impl Plan from AC; traceability coverage check Fewer late surprises; problems caught while they're cheap to fix % of items reworked after planning
One big batch — nominal estimates, no shared understanding Continuous refinement (~10% capacity) + async estimation + per-ticket framing Estimates grounded in real understanding; the short live meeting is decision-only refinement meeting length
Unstructured prose tickets — no traceable "why" Problem → Requirement → Non-Goal → AC with up-links and one canonical home per artifact Every ticket traces to a problem; coverage gaps and orphans are visible intake-to-sprint-ready lead time

⚠️ Honest caveats

  • Habit change takes 2–3 sprints to pay off — expect a temporary dip before the gains show.
  • T-shaped pairing reduces single-point risk but doesn't replace deep (e.g. Android) expertise overnight.
  • Moving exploration off the lead only works if Three Amigos triage genuinely covers feasibility.
  • Benefits here are projected from general industry practice — the ~40% defect-reduction figure cited in the proposal is indicative (secondary source) and was not validated against our own ticket/velocity data. Worth confirming the bottleneck quantitatively first.
